## What the guide represents

The [offline structure layout browser](structures/layout-browser.md) adds computed sizes, nested field offsets and configuration comparisons for a finite inventory of documented structures. Its generated coverage table records availability and failure reasons across four supplied iOS ARM64e base configurations, analyzed through separate header contexts. PAC features remain enabled; an explicit layout-only macro removes pointer-authentication qualifiers while retaining fields. The thread/IPv6 expansion inventories 66 roots, with 51 supported in all four configurations and 15 explicit boundaries. It adds ARM64 machine and saved-register state alongside IPv6 headers, options, routes, neighbor discovery and multicast records. `thread_group` remains opaque in the supplied headers; `in6_ifaddr` currently fails the source-identity check. Compiler/header provenance and layout validation are separate from the source-reading coverage below. This does not establish layout support for every documented structure, arbitrary product configurations or Apple-private compiler behavior.

Five [message and networking structure companions](structures/README.md#follow-messages-and-packets-into-their-storage) follow kmsg metadata and buffer layouts, socket allocation and protocol attachments, embedded sockbuf queues and counters, combined Internet/TCP PCB storage, and mbuf views with shared external backing. The [batch record](audits/first-release-expansion.md#29-message-and-networking-structures) connects field access to publication, references, locking, partial failure and final release. It also corrects the earlier TCP attachment description: this snapshot initializes an embedded TCP control block rather than allocating a second record. IOKit object companions, supporting VFS/event structures and exhaustive protocol/configuration/provider coverage remain further work.

Five [file and signature structure companions](structures/README.md#follow-descriptors-into-file-and-signature-storage) follow embedded descriptor tables and movable arrays, fileproc/fileglob sharing and I/O holds, vnode storage and identity, UBC/pager associations, and protected code-signature attachments. The [batch record](audits/first-release-expansion.md#28-file-descriptors-vnodes-and-signed-file-storage) connects fields to allocation, lookup, publication, synchronization, partial failure and staged release. The source review also corrects two older pager descriptions: the attribute handler consumes only copy strategy/cacheability, and the control-reference helper is a no-op. At that stage, message buffers, socket/PCB/mbuf and IOKit object companions remained candidates; exhaustive configurations and missing providers remained bounded.

Five [VM structure companions](structures/README.md#follow-virtual-memory-into-backing-and-translation-storage) follow maps and entries, VM objects and backing, resident-page descriptors, ARM64 pmap/table storage, and named memory entries/VM copies. The [batch record](audits/first-release-expansion.md#27-vm-structures-and-memory-capabilities) connects allocation and field access to publication, references, synchronization, failure and final release. Ordinary writable zones, PPL-protected storage and SPTM table ownership remain distinct. At that stage, descriptor/VFS, message-buffer, networking and IOKit structure companions remained candidates; exhaustive VM fields/configurations and external providers were not implied.

The [thread, credential and IPC structure batch](audits/first-release-expansion.md#26-thread-identity-credentials-and-ipc-structures) adds five companions to the [structure-reading route](structures/README.md). They follow thread/uthread/thread_ro storage and staged destruction, credential uniquing and two kinds of reference accounting, MAC label ownership and slots, task-local IPC tables and port/kobject representation. Field maps, exact excerpts and worked paths connect allocation, access, mutation, publication, locking, failure and final reclamation. The existing operation chapters remain the surrounding context; other structure families and exhaustive field/configuration coverage remain separate.

The [key-structures section](structures/README.md) begins with `proc`, `task` and their protected fields. Three companions follow allocation, initialization, validated access, selected writes and coordinated reclamation. The [structure-study record](audits/first-release-expansion.md#25-key-structures-proc-task-and-protected-updates) distinguishes ordinary shared-RO construction from kernel bootstrap and corpse tasks, and connects concrete updates to the legacy ARM/PPL and SPTM implementations. It extends existing lifecycle and allocator explanations without claiming complete field or configuration coverage.

The [IPsec-interface, MPTCP and process-inspection batch](audits/first-release-expansion.md#24-ipsec-interfaces-mptcp-and-process-inspection) adds three companions covering the remaining candidates from the preceding recommendation list. Parallel authors traced interface publication and packet ownership, multipath sessions above TCP subflows, and process identity/descriptor queries during concurrent changes. Independent reviews checked references, state transitions, worked examples and cleanup against the selected source; the [reading route](reading-guide.md#ipsec-interfaces-mptcp-and-process-inspection) connects each companion to its prerequisites. Broader protocol/configuration coverage, external implementations and the deferred debugger/kperf work remain separate.

The [two-batch follow-up](audits/first-release-expansion.md#22-executable-memory-synchronization-packet-policy-and-build-selection) adds five companions and deepens PF/IPsec in place. Parallel authors traced executable-memory policy, Mach synchronization, work-interval groups, flow-diversion providers, TCP/NAT state, IPv6 IPsec/request chains, and concrete ARM64 build/MIG selection. Independent reviews checked the resulting operations against source; [the reading route](reading-guide.md#executable-memory-synchronization-and-packet-policy) connects their prerequisites. Further debugger and kperf expansion is deferred by request.

The [argument, identity, instrumentation and packet-state pass](audits/first-release-expansion.md#21-arguments-identity-integrity-instrumentation-and-packet-state) adds twelve companions in the [requested reading order](reading-guide.md#argument-identity-and-lifetime-paths). These document concrete source operations and their boundaries: dispatch, VM validation, concurrent schedules, IOKit calls, exec, message/register integrity, sanitizer/coverage lifetimes, and BPF/PF/IPsec. Detailed source-test mappings state what the checked-in assertions establish; no target runtime results are inferred.

The guide was produced by inspecting the local `xnu-12377.121.6` tree. Three specialist agents explored the nine main subsystem areas in successive assignments; the coordinating review mapped the architecture, inspected build/observability/image-loading code, connected the paths, and checked navigation and consistency. No upstream web description was substituted for this checkout's implementation.

The IPC and ARM64 exception expansion assigned separate agents to namespace/right lifetimes, message transport, and hardware entry/return. Coordinating work connected kernel RPC, Mach exception delivery, and protected execution; independent reviews then checked the other authors' boundary descriptions. One official Arm reference supplies the architectural EL1↔EL2 contract, clearly separated from local XNU evidence and unavailable host/monitor implementations.

The VM expansion assigned separate agents to maps/faults, ARM64 pmap, and object/page/pager ownership. Coordinating work traced x86 pmap, end-to-end mapping examples, reclaim/compressor/swap, and kernel allocation. Independent reviews checked the other authors' mapping, fault-revalidation, pmap, reclaim, and allocator descriptions against the source. The resulting chapters distinguish common VM policy from the legacy ARM/PPL, SPTM, and x86 hardware backends.

The kernel-allocation expansion assigned separate agents to compiler type metadata and assignment policy, zone storage and specialized zones, and guarded kernel VM allocation. Coordinating work traced kalloc routing/free/realloc, IOKit/C++/BSD interfaces, restricted-page classification, and debugger/test entry points. Independent reviews checked other authors' allocation and release paths, type compatibility, cache accounting, address retention, and backend distinctions. The guide separates type placement from free-time validation, virtual-address reuse, physical backing, and protected mutation.

The protected-execution expansion assigned separate agents to SPTM, TXM, and Exclaves execution. The file-cache/signature expansion added seven implementation chapters. The resulting route distinguishes resident bytes, signature metadata, cached integrity results and executable mapping permission.

“Deep path review” means reading named structures, selected function bodies, adjacent invariants, callers/dispatch tables, and relevant cleanup paths. It does not mean every branch of a large file was audited. Each subsystem index provides a more specific sources-and-limits table.

Of those pages, 349 are explanatory guides/catalogs/build instructions and ten are coverage/audit/generated-navigation records. These counts use the current XNU-root source-link inventory; project tools are not counted as XNU sources. They are citation counts, not reviewed-line or implementation-depth percentages.

The review separates existing mechanism coverage from residual multicast/router-prefix, PF/IPsec, sanitizer/coverage and transport-overlay gaps.

The [VM/IPC complex-flow batch](audits/first-release-expansion.md#18-vm-fault-upl-and-complex-ipc-state-transitions) expands existing object/fault and port-lifetime explanations and adds three companions for UPL completion, complex-message conversion and importance propagation. Parallel research and source cross-review connect preservation and retry to partial resource acquisition, publication and cleanup. It records specific unresolved boundaries rather than treating an error return as proof of full rollback; no kernel tests were executed.
